\section{Optuna results}
\label{sec:optuna}

This section presents and discusses 4 of Optuna's visualizations, chosen
due to their perceived relevance with respect to the work at hand:

\begin{multicols}{2}
\begin{enumerate}
    \item Optimization History \\
    Scatter plot of the objective function value across trials.

    \item Parameter Importances \\
    Horizontal bar plot of parameter importance.
    Importance here is understood as the fraction of the objective function reference the parameter explains \cite{pmlr-v32-hutter14}.
    
    \item Slice \\
    Scatter plots the objective function with respect to each parameter separately.

    \item Terminator Improvement \\
    Objective improvement potentials, 
    i.e., it estimates how much further the objective could be minimized if the study continued. 
\end{enumerate}
\end{multicols}

\subsection{Feedforward Neural Network}

Optuna's visualizations for the FNN are presented in \cref{fig:optuna_fnn}.
Optimization history and terminator improvement suggest that the study was thorough.
%
As the slice plot shows, the number of hidden layers correlated positively with coefficient with error rate;
this is probably due to the classic bias vs. variance trade-off associated with model complexity.
The bigger surprise comes from the importance of the hidden layer activation function:
ReLU dominates the sigmoid in potential to be associated with a good trial. 
%
This could be due to the different ranges of each feature, which when to the weights, saturate the sigmoid.
Batch normalization, for example, could do away with this contrast.

\subsection{Decision Tree}

Optuna's visualizations for the DT are presented in \cref{fig:optuna_dt}.
Optimization history and terminator improvement suggest that the study was thorough,
but it is surprising that the importance of the tree's depth is smaller that the tree's criterion.
%
The slice plots suggest that, given a criterion, the tree's error rate will be smaller the shallower its depth:
there is a positive correlation between the error rate and model complexity in the max depth slice plot,
which most likely indicates the classic bias vs. variance trade-off of a model.

\subsection{Support Vector Machine}

Optuna's visualizations for the SVM are presented in \cref{fig:optuna_svm}.
Optimization history and terminator improvement suggest that the study was thorough,
but it is surprising that the regularization constant's importance is greater than the kernel's.
%
The slice plots show that, indeed, the error rate is more sensitive to changes in the constant than the kernel.
%
They also show that the linear kernel also achieved 0 error rate for at least one trial (notedly, with a high regularization constant), 
while the RBF and sigmoid kernels yelded relatively high errors.
%
This indicates that, in principle, the vector space that best separates the samples is not complicated. 
The ease of class separation should explain, at least partially, why changes in kernel 
don't affect the objective function as much as changes in the regularization constant.
